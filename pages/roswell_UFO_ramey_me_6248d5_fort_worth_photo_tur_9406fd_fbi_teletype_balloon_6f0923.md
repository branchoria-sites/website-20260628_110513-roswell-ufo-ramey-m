## Introduction

The Fort Worth press photographs were only one part of the military's rapid response to the Roswell announcement. At almost the same time that reporters were being shown [balloon debris]({{ 'balloon-debris/' | relative_url }}) at Eighth Air Force headquarters, an internal FBI teletype recorded the same basic explanation moving through official government channels. That single-page message is important because it shows that the balloon-and-radar-reflector account was not confined to newspaper headlines or a staged photo opportunity. Instead, it was being communicated between military officers and the FBI on 8 July 1947, providing an independent documentary record of how the official explanation spread beyond the press.

## What the Dallas teletype reported

On 8 July 1947, the FBI's Dallas Field Office sent a teletype to FBI Headquarters and the Bureau's Cincinnati office after receiving information by telephone from Eighth Air Force headquarters in Fort Worth. The message stated that an object "purporting to be a flying disc" had been recovered near Roswell, but immediately added details pointing towards a conventional explanation. According to the military source, the object was hexagonal in shape, suspended from a balloon by cable, and attached to a balloon approximately 20 feet in diameter. The teletype further reported that the recovered object resembled "a high altitude weather balloon with a radar reflector". It also noted that the material was being flown to [Wright Field]({{ 'wright-field/' | relative_url }}) in Ohio for examination and concluded that the FBI would take no further investigative action. <span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://vault.fbi.gov/Roswell%20UFO" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: vault.fbi.gov">[FBI]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">vault.fbi.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Roswell UFOOn</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Roswell UFOOn July 8, 1947, the FBI Dallas Field Office sent a teletype regarding a “flying disc” that resembled a high altitude weath...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: July 8, 1947</span></span></span>

Several aspects of the document make it historically significant. It was created on the day of the Roswell announcement, before later decades of controversy, and it records what military officials were communicating internally rather than what journalists chose to print. Unlike later recollections, it is a contemporaneous administrative record preserved in FBI files and later released through the Freedom of Information Act. <span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://vault.fbi.gov/Roswell%20UFO" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: vault.fbi.gov">[FBI]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">vault.fbi.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Roswell UFOOn</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Roswell UFOOn July 8, 1947, the FBI Dallas Field Office sent a teletype regarding a “flying disc” that resembled a high altitude weath...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: July 8, 1947</span></span></span>

## How Eighth Air Force described the object

The wording of the FBI teletype closely matches the explanation emerging from Fort Worth during the famous [photo session]({{ 'photo-session/' | relative_url }}). Rather than describing a metallic craft, the Eighth Air Force characterised the recovered material as something suspended beneath a balloon and resembling a weather balloon equipped with a radar reflector. That description is notable because radar targets of the period commonly consisted of lightweight wooden sticks and reflective foil arranged in angular shapes, producing debris unlike an ordinary latex balloon alone. <span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://vault.fbi.gov/Roswell%20UFO" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: vault.fbi.gov">[FBI]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">vault.fbi.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Roswell UFOOn</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Roswell UFOOn July 8, 1947, the FBI Dallas Field Office sent a teletype regarding a “flying disc” that resembled a high altitude weath...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: July 8, 1947</span></span></span>

The document also shows that the military explanation was not yet entirely settled. While reporting that the object resembled a weather balloon with a radar reflector, the teletype added that conversations with Wright Field "had not borne out this belief." Historians have interpreted this wording in different ways. Some see it as evidence of uncertainty during a rapidly developing event, while others argue it reflects routine discussion before specialists examined the debris. Whatever its precise meaning, the sentence demonstrates that information was still evolving as reports moved between commands on 8 July rather than emerging as a perfectly coordinated [narrative]({{ 'narrative/' | relative_url }}). <span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://vault.fbi.gov/Roswell%20UFO" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: vault.fbi.gov">[FBI]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">vault.fbi.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Roswell UFOOn</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Roswell UFOOn July 8, 1947, the FBI Dallas Field Office sent a teletype regarding a “flying disc” that resembled a high altitude weath...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: July 8, 1947</span></span></span>

## Why internal records matter beside press photos

The Fort Worth photographs remain the best-known visual record of the official explanation, but the FBI teletype adds something different. Photographs show what reporters were shown; the teletype shows what one federal agency was officially told by another. Together they indicate that the balloon explanation was being disseminated simultaneously through public relations and inter-agency communications.

This distinction matters because the FBI was not conducting an independent investigation into the Roswell debris. The Bureau simply recorded information received from the military and documented that it would take no further action. That limited role reduces the temptation to treat the document as proof of either extraterrestrial claims or later Air Force explanations. Instead, it serves as evidence of the government's communication process on the day itself. <span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://vault.fbi.gov/Roswell%20UFO" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: vault.fbi.gov">[FBI]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">vault.fbi.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Roswell UFOOn</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Roswell UFOOn July 8, 1947, the FBI Dallas Field Office sent a teletype regarding a “flying disc” that resembled a high altitude weath...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: July 8, 1947</span></span></span>

The teletype also helps explain why the balloon account spread so quickly. Once Eighth Air Force headquarters had adopted that description, the explanation moved not only through newspaper stories and photographs but through formal government messaging. The document therefore captures the official narrative at the moment it was being transmitted beyond Fort Worth to other federal offices.

## How later investigations used the teletype

When the Government Accountability Office reviewed surviving Roswell records during its 1994–95 investigation, the FBI teletype became one of the few authentic federal documents created during the original incident. The GAO confirmed the message's authenticity through the FBI and reproduced its contents as part of its search for surviving government records. Investigators noted that they found no corresponding Wright Field records documenting examination of recovered debris, highlighting both the value and the limits of the surviving documentary trail. <span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://sgp.fas.org/othergov/roswell.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: sgp.fas.org">[FAS Project on Government Secrecy]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">sgp.fas.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">FAS Project on Government SecrecyGAO Report on Roswell, NM UFO CrashDuring our review of records at FBI headquarters, we found a July 8...</span></span></span>

Critics of that conclusion, however, have pointed out that the teletype itself does not mention Project Mogul and merely records what Eighth Air Force personnel believed or reported on 8 July 1947.
