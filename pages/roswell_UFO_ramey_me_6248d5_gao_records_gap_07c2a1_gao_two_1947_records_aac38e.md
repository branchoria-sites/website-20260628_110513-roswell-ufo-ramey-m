## Introduction

When the U.S. General Accounting Office (GAO, now the Government Accountability Office) completed its Roswell records investigation in 1995, it reached a strikingly narrow conclusion: among the government records it examined, only two surviving documents created in 1947 clearly referred to the Roswell incident itself. Those records were a July 1947 history of the combined 509th Bomb Group and Roswell Army Air Field, and a July 8, 1947 FBI teletype. Both point toward balloon-related debris rather than an exotic craft. At the same time, neither document is detailed enough to settle every later dispute, including claims made about the wording of the [Ramey memo]({{ 'ramey-memo/' | relative_url }}) visible in press photographs. The value of the two records lies in what they can demonstrate directly—and in recognising the limits of what they can demonstrate.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.gao.gov/products/nsiad" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: gao.gov">[GAO]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">gao.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">nsiad</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Results of a Search for Records Concerning the 1947...GAO provided information on the 1947 weather balloon crash at Roswell Army Air...</span></span></span>

## The 509th History and Its Balloon Wording

The first record identified by GAO was the official July 1947 history of the combined 509th Bomb Group and Roswell Army Air Field. According to the GAO report, the document recorded that the base public information office had been occupied with inquiries about a reported “flying disc” supposedly in the possession of the 509th Bomb Group. It then stated that the object “turned out to be a radar tracking balloon.” GAO further noted that the commanding officer certified the history as a complete and accurate account of the base's activities during that month.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.gao.gov/assets/nsiad-95-187.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: gao.gov">[GAO]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">gao.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">nsiad</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">NSIAD-95-187 Government Records28 Jul 1995 — identified the July 8, 1947, FBI teletype message discussing the recovery near Roswell of...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: July 8, 1947</span></span></span>

This record matters because it was not written decades later during the modern Roswell controversy. It was a contemporaneous military administrative document produced in July 1947. As evidence, it shows that within the Roswell command structure, the official explanation recorded at the time was balloon-related.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.gao.gov/assets/nsiad-95-187.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: gao.gov">[GAO]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">gao.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">nsiad</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">NSIAD-95-187 Government Records28 Jul 1995 — identified the July 8, 1947, FBI teletype message discussing the recovery near Roswell of...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: July 8, 1947</span></span></span>

However, the document's evidential reach is limited. It does not describe the debris in detail. It does not explain how the balloon identification was made. It does not discuss intelligence projects, recovered materials, bodies, or any of the claims that emerged in later Roswell narratives. It records an official conclusion, not the investigative process behind that conclusion.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.gao.gov/assets/nsiad-95-187.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: gao.gov">[GAO]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">gao.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">nsiad</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">NSIAD-95-187 Government Records28 Jul 1995 — identified the July 8, 1947, FBI teletype message discussing the recovery near Roswell of...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: July 8, 1947</span></span></span>

For that reason, the history is best understood as proof of what Roswell Army Air Field officially documented in July 1947—not as a comprehensive explanation of every event connected with the case.

## The July 8 FBI Teletype in Context

The second record was an FBI teletype dated July 8, 1947. The message reported information received from military sources concerning an object recovered near Roswell. According to the document, the object resembled a high-altitude weather balloon with a radar reflector. The teletype also described the recovered item as disc-shaped or “flying disc” related, while simultaneously conveying the military assessment that it matched balloon-and-radar-reflector equipment.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.gao.gov/assets/nsiad-95-187.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: gao.gov">[GAO+2FBI]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">gao.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">nsiad</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">NSIAD-95-187 Government Records28 Jul 1995 — identified the July 8, 1947, FBI teletype message discussing the recovery near Roswell of...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: July 8, 1947</span></span></span>

The FBI message is important because it was created during the crucial period when news of the Roswell recovery was first spreading. Unlike later recollections, it captures what federal officials were communicating in real time. The document demonstrates that, on 8 July 1947, military personnel were already presenting a balloon explanation to the FBI.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://vault.fbi.gov/Roswell%20UFO" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: vault.fbi.gov">[FBI]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">vault.fbi.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Roswell UFOOn</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Roswell UFOOn July 8, 1947, the FBI Dallas Field Office sent a teletype regarding a “flying disc” that resembled a high altitude weath...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: July 8, 1947</span></span></span>

Yet the teletype is often misunderstood. It was not an FBI forensic report. The Bureau was relaying information obtained from military contacts and explicitly noted that it was not conducting further investigation. The message therefore provides evidence of what military authorities were saying at the time, but not an independent FBI determination of the debris' identity.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.gao.gov/assets/nsiad-95-187.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: gao.gov">[GAO]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">gao.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">nsiad</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">NSIAD-95-187 Government Records28 Jul 1995 — identified the July 8, 1947, FBI teletype message discussing the recovery near Roswell of...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: July 8, 1947</span></span></span>

The wording also leaves room for debate over exactly what officials had recovered. The document reports resemblance to a balloon and radar reflector; it does not provide photographs, technical measurements, or a detailed inventory. As a result, supporters and critics of the conventional explanation have both cited the teletype, although the plain language of the message clearly points toward balloon-related material rather than an extraordinary craft.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://vault.fbi.gov/Roswell%20UFO" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: vault.fbi.gov">[FBI]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">vault.fbi.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Roswell UFOOn</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Roswell UFOOn July 8, 1947, the FBI Dallas Field Office sent a teletype regarding a “flying disc” that resembled a high altitude weath...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: July 8, 1947</span></span></span>

## Where These Records Help

Taken together, the two documents establish several points with unusual strength.

First, both records originated in 1947 rather than decades later. They therefore avoid many of the memory problems that affect retrospective testimony.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.gao.gov/assets/nsiad-95-187.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: gao.gov">[GAO]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">gao.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">nsiad</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">NSIAD-95-187 Government Records28 Jul 1995 — identified the July 8, 1947, FBI teletype message discussing the recovery near Roswell of...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: July 8, 1947</span></span></span>

Second, the two records are independent in origin. One came from military unit history files, the other from FBI communications. Despite different bureaucratic paths, both point toward balloon-related debris.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.gao.gov/assets/nsiad-95-187.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: gao.gov">[GAO+2gao.justia.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">gao.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">nsiad</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">NSIAD-95-187 Government Records28 Jul 1995 — identified the July 8, 1947, FBI teletype message discussing the recovery near Roswell of...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: July 8, 1947</span></span></span>

Third, they show that the balloon explanation was not simply invented many years later. Whatever one thinks of later Air Force interpretations such as [Project Mogul]({{ 'project-mogul/' | relative_url }}), contemporaneous government records already connected the Roswell recovery with balloon equipment and [radar reflectors]({{ 'radar-reflectors/' | relative_url }}).<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.gao.gov/assets/nsiad-95-187.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: gao.gov">[GAO+2DAF History]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">gao.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">nsiad</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">NSIAD-95-187 Government Records28 Jul 1995 — identified the July 8, 1947, FBI teletype message discussing the recovery near Roswell of...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: July 8, 1947</span></span></span>

These are significant findings because they place hard documentary constraints on claims about what the government was saying in July 1947.

## Where the Records Stop

The importance of the two records is sometimes exaggerated. They do not prove every aspect of the later Air Force reconstruction. They do not identify a specific balloon train, serial number, launch record, or recovery chain. They do not explain why some Roswell-related record series were later destroyed or missing when GAO conducted its search.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://ufotransparency.com/files/1995-07-28-gao-roswell-report-nsiad-95-187-gao-nsiad-95-187-roswell-1995" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ufotransparency.com">[UFO Transparency]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ufotransparency.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">1995 07 28 gao roswell report nsiad 95 187 gao nsiad 95 187 roswell 1995</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UFO TransparencyResults of a Search for Records Concerning the 1947 Crash...28 Jul 1995 — The GAO&#x27;s 1995 audit of federal records relate...</span></span></span>

Most importantly for debates surrounding the Ramey memo, neither document can resolve disputed interpretations of tiny, partially legible text appearing in a press photograph. The [GAO records]({{ 'gao-records/' | relative_url }}) neither confirm nor refute proposed [readings]({{ 'readings/' | relative_url }}) of the memo. They simply provide two contemporaneous documentary data points indicating that official channels in July 1947 were describing the recovered material as balloon-related.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.gao.gov/assets/nsiad-95-187.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: gao.gov">[GAO]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">gao.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">nsiad</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">NSIAD-95-187 Government Records28 Jul 1995 — identified the July 8, 1947, FBI teletype message discussing the recovery near Roswell of...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: July 8, 1947</span></span></span>

That distinction matters. The two records are strongest when used for what they actually show: that surviving 1947 government documentation points toward a balloon and radar-reflector explanation. They become much weaker when asked to answer broader questions that the documents themselves never address.

## What the Two Records Really Prove

The most defensible conclusion is a narrow one. The GAO's search found only two clearly relevant surviving 1947 government records, and both describe the Roswell recovery in balloon-related terms. That gives historians a contemporaneous documentary foundation that cannot be dismissed as a later invention.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.gao.gov/products/nsiad" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: gao.gov">[GAO+2GAO]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">gao.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">nsiad</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Results of a Search for Records Concerning the 1947...GAO provided information on the 1947 weather balloon crash at Roswell Army Air...</span></span></span>

At the same time, the records are brief, administrative, and incomplete. They document an official understanding of the incident rather than providing a full evidentiary file. As a result, they are highly relevant to the Roswell debate but insufficient on their own to settle every controversy surrounding the case, including claims built around the [Ramey]({{ 'ramey/' | relative_url }}) memo. Their greatest value is not that they answer every question, but that they define the narrow set of contemporaneous documentary facts that can be stated with confidence.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.gao.gov/assets/nsiad-95-187.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: gao.gov">[GAO+2FBI]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">gao.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">nsiad</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">NSIAD-95-187 Government Records28 Jul 1995 — identified the July 8, 1947, FBI teletype message discussing the recovery near Roswell of...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: July 8, 1947</span></span></span>
