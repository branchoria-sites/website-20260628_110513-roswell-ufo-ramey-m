## Introduction

The official Roswell explanation ultimately rests on a surprisingly specific claim: that the debris recovered on the Foster ranch came from a single [Project Mogul]({{ 'project-mogul/' | relative_url }}) balloon train launched by New York University researchers from Alamogordo in June 1947 and never recovered. That candidate is usually identified as NYU Flight 4. If Flight 4 can plausibly account for the debris field, the later Air Force explanation gains an important evidential foundation. If Flight 4 cannot be shown to have existed, flown where claimed, or remained unrecovered, a central pillar of the Mogul hypothesis becomes weaker. As a result, debates over Roswell often focus less on exotic theories than on a narrower historical question: what do the surviving Flight 4 records actually show?<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.dafhistory.af.mil/Portals/16/documents/AFD-101201-038.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: dafhistory.af.mil">[DAF History]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">dafhistory.af.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">DAF HistoryThe Roswell ReportThe importance of attachment 32, Lieutenant McAndrew&#x27;s synopsis, derives from his description of Project MOG...</span></span></span>

## What the Flight 4 records say

Project Mogul was still in an experimental phase during the summer of 1947. The New York University balloon team conducted a series of launches from Alamogordo Army Air Field as part of efforts to develop a high-altitude acoustic detection system for monitoring Soviet nuclear tests. Surviving project records show that a launch designated Flight 4 was scheduled during this period and later became the focus of Roswell investigations.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.dafhistory.af.mil/Portals/16/documents/AFD-101201-038.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: dafhistory.af.mil">[DAF History]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">dafhistory.af.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">DAF HistoryThe Roswell ReportThe importance of attachment 32, Lieutenant McAndrew&#x27;s synopsis, derives from his description of Project MOG...</span></span></span>

The Air Force's 1994 and 1995 Roswell investigations treated Flight 4 as the most likely source of the debris found by rancher W. W. “Mack” Brazel. According to that reconstruction, the balloon train launched on 4 June 1947, travelled generally toward the Corona area northeast of Alamogordo, and was never recovered. Because the equipment was classified, personnel at Roswell Army Air Field would not necessarily have recognised its purpose even if they encountered its remains. U.S. Department of War+2DAF History<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://media.defense.gov/2010/Oct/27/2001330219/-1/-1/0/AFD-101027-030.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: media.defense.gov">[media.defense.gov]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">media.defense.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">AFD 101027 030</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Department of WarU.S. Air Force: &quot;The Roswell Report: Case Closed&quot;The 1994 Air Force report determined that project MOGUL was responsible...</span></span></span>

However, the documentary record is not entirely straightforward. Critics of the Mogul explanation have pointed to entries in project notes, particularly those associated with project scientist Albert Crary, arguing that Flight 4 may have been cancelled or that the launch configuration later described by the Air Force differed from what actually flew. These disputes emerged after researchers examined surviving field logs more closely than had been done during earlier Roswell investigations.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.researchgate.net/publication/362854511_A_Grounded_Theory_Update_on_the_Roswell_UFO_Incident" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: researchgate.net">[ResearchGate]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">researchgate.net</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">A Grounded Theory Update on the Roswell UFO IncidentProject Mogul does not provide the ultimate answer. As a result, Flight 4 occupies an unusual position in Roswell history. It is neither a fully documented flight with complete recovery records nor an entirely hypothetical event. Instead, historians are working with incomplete launch records, field notes, later recollections and Air Force reconstructions assembled decades after the fact.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.dafhistory.af.mil/Portals/16/documents/AFD-101201-038.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: dafhistory.af.mil">[DAF History]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">dafhistory.af.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">DAF HistoryThe Roswell ReportThe importance of attachment 32, Lieutenant McAndrew&#x27;s synopsis, derives from his description of Project MOG...</span></span></span>

## How the projected path fits the ranch recovery

The strongest argument for Flight 4 as the Roswell debris source is geographical rather than purely documentary. Multiple reconstructions of the flight path place the balloon train moving east-northeast from Alamogordo toward the Corona region, which lies near the area where Brazel reported finding scattered debris.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://skepticalinquirer.org/2017/12/the-roswell-incident-at-70-facts-not-myths/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: skepticalinquirer.org">[Skeptical Inquirer+2Smithsonian Magazine]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">skepticalinquirer.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Skeptical InquirerThe Roswell Incident at 70: Facts, Not Myths4. The research team launched NYU Flight #4 on June 4, 1947, from Alamogord...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: June 4, 1947</span></span></span>

This fit matters because the debris descriptions reported in 1947 were not limited to the remains of a simple weather balloon. Witnesses described lightweight materials spread over a relatively broad area. The Mogul explanation argues that such observations are more consistent with a long balloon train incorporating balloons, radar-reflector components, cords, tape and associated equipment than with a single meteorological balloon. Air Force investigators emphasised that Mogul arrays could extend hundreds of feet in length and fragment into numerous lightweight pieces when descending.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://media.defense.gov/2010/Oct/27/2001330219/-1/-1/0/AFD-101027-030.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: media.defense.gov">[U.S. Department of War]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">media.defense.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">AFD 101027 030</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Department of WarU.S. Air Force: &quot;The Roswell Report: Case Closed&quot;The 1994 Air Force report determined that project MOGUL was responsible...</span></span></span>

Supporters of the Flight 4 identification also note that other June 1947 Mogul flights were recovered, whereas Flight 4 was allegedly lost. That circumstance provides a mechanism by which debris could remain on a remote ranch until discovered weeks later. The absence of a documented recovery therefore becomes part of the explanation rather than a problem for it.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.wired.com/story/roswell-aliens-fermi-paradox" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: wired.com">[WIRED]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">wired.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">However, an examination reveals a confluence of secret government projects and Cold War era activities rather than extraterrestrial invol...</span></span></span>

The geographical fit is not perfect proof. Weather conditions, tracking limitations and gaps in the surviving records make exact reconstruction difficult. Nevertheless, among known Mogul launches, Flight 4 has generally been regarded by official investigators as the candidate whose projected trajectory most closely aligns with the area where debris was later reported. ## What the missing-recovery claim can and cannot prove

A common misunderstanding is that because Flight 4 was reportedly never recovered, it must therefore have been the Roswell object. The historical evidence does not support such certainty.

What the missing-recovery claim can establish is relatively modest. It shows that a classified balloon programme was operating in New Mexico during the relevant period; that at least one launch is believed to have escaped recovery; and that a plausible route exists between a Mogul launch site and the region where debris was found. Those facts make Flight 4 a credible candidate. DAF History+2U.S. Department of War<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.dafhistory.af.mil/Portals/16/documents/AFD-101201-038.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: dafhistory.af.mil">[dafhistory.af.mil]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">dafhistory.af.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">DAF HistoryThe Roswell ReportThe importance of attachment 32, Lieutenant McAndrew&#x27;s synopsis, derives from his description of Project MOG...</span></span></span>

What the claim cannot establish is a direct chain of custody between the launch and the debris collected by Brazel. No surviving document records a recovery team finding Flight 4 wreckage on the Foster ranch. No known photograph identifies recovered debris as belonging specifically to that flight.
