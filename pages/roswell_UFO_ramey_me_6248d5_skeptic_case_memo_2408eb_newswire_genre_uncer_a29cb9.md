## Introduction

One of the less discussed reasons sceptics reject the [Ramey memo]({{ 'ramey-memo/' | relative_url }}) as proof of a crashed extraterrestrial craft is that nobody knows with certainty what kind of document it was in the first place. Even before the debate reaches specific words such as “victims of the wreck”, there is a more basic question: was General Roger [Ramey]({{ 'ramey/' | relative_url }}) holding a secret military message, a routine office communication, or simply a press-wire copy related to the rapidly developing Roswell story?

The uncertainty matters because the memo’s evidential value depends heavily on its genre. A classified operational message and a newswire bulletin are interpreted very differently. If the paper was a wire-service report summarising events already circulating among journalists, then apparent references to Roswell would not automatically represent private military admissions. For sceptics, the unresolved question of what the document actually was weakens attempts to treat it as a decisive piece of evidence.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://sites.libraries.uta.edu/roswell/node/21" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: sites.libraries.uta.edu">[UT Arlington Libraries]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">sites.libraries.uta.edu</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UT Arlington LibrariesDeciphering the Ramey Memo &#124; Roswell UFO Incident18 Jan 2017 — It is dated July 8, 1947 and was transmitted over th...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: July 8, 1947</span></span></span>

## Was the memo even a military secret?

The photograph itself does not identify the document. It shows Ramey holding a sheet of paper while posing with debris in Fort Worth on 8 July 1947. The image preserves only a small and partially legible portion of the page. Researchers therefore have had to infer both the text and the document type from limited visual clues.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://sites.libraries.uta.edu/roswell/node/21" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: sites.libraries.uta.edu">[UT Arlington Libraries]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">sites.libraries.uta.edu</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UT Arlington LibrariesDeciphering the Ramey Memo &#124; Roswell UFO Incident18 Jan 2017 — It is dated July 8, 1947 and was transmitted over th...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: July 8, 1947</span></span></span>

A key point often overlooked in popular discussions is that the surviving photograph does not establish classification markings, routing information, security labels, or other features that would clearly identify the paper as a secret military communication. Because those indicators are not securely readable, competing interpretations remain possible.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://sites.libraries.uta.edu/roswell/node/21" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: sites.libraries.uta.edu">[UT Arlington Libraries]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">sites.libraries.uta.edu</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UT Arlington LibrariesDeciphering the Ramey Memo &#124; Roswell UFO Incident18 Jan 2017 — It is dated July 8, 1947 and was transmitted over th...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: July 8, 1947</span></span></span>

The University of Texas at Arlington's Roswell research project, which has closely examined the photograph and associated materials, notes that the document has never been definitively deciphered. That leaves open multiple possibilities regarding its purpose and origin.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://sites.libraries.uta.edu/roswell/node/21" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: sites.libraries.uta.edu">[UT Arlington Libraries]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">sites.libraries.uta.edu</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UT Arlington LibrariesDeciphering the Ramey Memo &#124; Roswell UFO Incident18 Jan 2017 — It is dated July 8, 1947 and was transmitted over th...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: July 8, 1947</span></span></span>

## Johnson's changing account

Discussion of the document's nature is closely tied to recollections by photographer J. Bond [Johnson]({{ 'johnson/' | relative_url }}), who took the famous Fort Worth photographs.

Over the years, Johnson offered different descriptions of what Ramey was holding. Some researchers have cited statements suggesting that Johnson believed the paper related directly to military communications. Others have noted that Johnson also discussed the possibility that the sheet could have been connected to press operations and news distribution. The variations in recollection are not surprising given that the interviews occurred decades after the event, but they complicate efforts to treat any single description as definitive.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://kevinrandle.blogspot.com/2009/10/ufos-ramey-memo-j-bond-johnson-and-me.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: kevinrandle.blogspot.com">[Kevin Randle&#x27;s Blog]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">kevinrandle.blogspot.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Kevin Randle&#x27;s Blog UFOs, the Ramey Memo, J</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Bond Johnson and Me9 Oct 2009 — Lance asked Kevin Randle the question, is there anything there in the Fort Worth Ramey photos that indica...</span></span></span>

For sceptics, the significance of these shifting accounts is not that Johnson was unreliable, but that his testimony does not conclusively settle the genre question. If the eyewitness closest to the photograph left room for different interpretations, then the image itself must carry most of the evidential burden—and the image is precisely where ambiguity remains strongest.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://sites.libraries.uta.edu/roswell/node/21" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: sites.libraries.uta.edu">[UT Arlington Libraries]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">sites.libraries.uta.edu</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UT Arlington LibrariesDeciphering the Ramey Memo &#124; Roswell UFO Incident18 Jan 2017 — It is dated July 8, 1947 and was transmitted over th...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: July 8, 1947</span></span></span>

## How a newswire could fit the scene

One of the most important pieces of evidence supporting the newswire possibility comes from the historical media environment of July 1947.

The Roswell story spread rapidly through wire services. Newspapers, radio stations and military public-information offices were exchanging bulletins as the “flying disc” announcement became national news. Wire-service copies were routinely handled, passed around and read by officials and journalists alike. In that context, it would not have been unusual for a senior officer to have a wire report in his office while reporters and photographers were present.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Press-Radio_War" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">The Press-Radio War</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The Press-Radio War</span></span></span>

The UTA Roswell research project has highlighted a particularly relevant detail: one candidate document associated with the photograph was an International News Service (INS) wire transmission dated 8 July 1947 and sent late that evening. Researchers have explored whether the visible paper could have been a news-service copy rather than an internal military message. The fact that a contemporaneous wire document exists and fits the time frame means the newswire hypothesis cannot simply be dismissed.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://sites.libraries.uta.edu/roswell/node/21" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: sites.libraries.uta.edu">[UT Arlington Libraries]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">sites.libraries.uta.edu</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UT Arlington LibrariesDeciphering the Ramey Memo &#124; Roswell UFO Incident18 Jan 2017 — It is dated July 8, 1947 and was transmitted over th...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: July 8, 1947</span></span></span>

From a sceptical perspective, this possibility changes the meaning of any partially readable Roswell-related words. If a document is a press-wire summary, then references to discs, wreckage, recovery efforts or official statements may merely reflect information already circulating in the news cycle. Such wording would not necessarily reveal what military officials privately believed.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://sites.libraries.uta.edu/roswell/node/21" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: sites.libraries.uta.edu">[UT Arlington Libraries]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">sites.libraries.uta.edu</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UT Arlington LibrariesDeciphering the Ramey Memo &#124; Roswell UFO Incident18 Jan 2017 — It is dated July 8, 1947 and was transmitted over th...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: July 8, 1947</span></span></span>

## Why genre changes evidential value

The debate is not merely academic. The document's genre directly affects how historians should interpret its contents.

Consider two scenarios:

* **Scenario A:** the paper is a classified operational message discussing a recovery operation.
* **Scenario B:** the paper is a wire-service report summarising public claims and official responses.

The same phrase could have dramatically different significance in each case. A reference to a “wreck”, for example, would be far more consequential in a private operational document than in a news bulletin reporting what others were saying. The words alone cannot carry the same weight unless the document type is first established.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://sites.libraries.uta.edu/roswell/node/21" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: sites.libraries.uta.edu">[UT Arlington Libraries]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">sites.libraries.uta.edu</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UT Arlington LibrariesDeciphering the Ramey Memo &#124; Roswell UFO Incident18 Jan 2017 — It is dated July 8, 1947 and was transmitted over th...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: July 8, 1947</span></span></span>

This is one reason sceptics frequently argue that proponents place too much emphasis on isolated words while paying too little attention to document [provenance]({{ 'provenance/' | relative_url }}) and context. In historical analysis, identifying a document's purpose is often as important as identifying its text. A newspaper clipping, a teletype bulletin, a briefing note and an operational order can all contain similar language while serving entirely different functions.
