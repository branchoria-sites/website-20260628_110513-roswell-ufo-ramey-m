## Introduction

John [Kirby]({{ 'kirby/' | relative_url }})’s alternative reading of the [Ramey memo]({{ 'ramey-memo/' | relative_url }}) is significant because it aligns more closely with the way the Roswell incident was discussed in July 1947 than with the body-recovery narratives that emerged decades later. In Kirby’s interpretation, a key phrase refers to “the remains of the material you commanded we fly,” shifting attention away from alleged crash victims and towards recovered physical debris. That distinction matters because the earliest Roswell records—newspaper reports, military statements, and the FBI teletype sent on 8 July 1947—focused overwhelmingly on the recovery and identification of material objects rather than bodies or occupants. <span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.researchgate.net/profile/James-Houran/publication/228706129_A_Message_in_a_Bottle_Confounds_in_Deciphering_the_Ramey_Memo_from_the_Roswell_UFO_Case/links/0deec527833e585cd6000000/A-Message-in-a-Bottle-Confounds-in-Deciphering-the-Ramey-Memo-from-the-Roswell-UFO-Case.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: researchgate.net">[ResearchGate]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">researchgate.net</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Research Gate&#x27;&#x27;A Message in a Bottle:&quot; Confounds in Deciphering ...said, according to Kirby, “are the remains of the material you command</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">ResearchGate&#x27;&#x27;A Message in a Bottle:&quot; Confounds in Deciphering ...said, according to Kirby, “are the remains of the material you commande...</span></span></span>

## The Early Records Focused on Recovered Material

The first Roswell news cycle was fundamentally a debris story. Rancher W.W. “Mac” Brazel described finding scattered fragments consisting of rubber, foil-like material, paper, tape, and sticks. Contemporary newspaper accounts emphasised the physical characteristics of the wreckage and the military’s efforts to identify it. They did not describe alien bodies, crash survivors, or biological remains. <span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roswell_incident" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Roswell incident</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Roswell incident</span></span></span>

Even after the Army Air Forces briefly announced possession of a “flying disc,” attention remained fixed on what had been recovered from the ground. Jesse Marcel’s contemporaneous descriptions likewise dealt with fragments and components rather than occupants. The central question in July 1947 was what the material was, not who might have been inside it. <span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roswell_incident" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Roswell incident</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Roswell incident</span></span></span>

Against that backdrop, Kirby’s wording appears historically compatible with the surviving record. A phrase referring to “remains of the material” sounds like the language of debris recovery. It treats the recovered object as a collection of remnants or fragments requiring identification, which mirrors the way witnesses and officials discussed the discovery during the original event window. <span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.researchgate.net/profile/James-Houran/publication/228706129_A_Message_in_a_Bottle_Confounds_in_Deciphering_the_Ramey_Memo_from_the_Roswell_UFO_Case/links/0deec527833e585cd6000000/A-Message-in-a-Bottle-Confounds-in-Deciphering-the-Ramey-Memo-from-the-Roswell-UFO-Case.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: researchgate.net">[ResearchGate]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">researchgate.net</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Research Gate&#x27;&#x27;A Message in a Bottle:&quot; Confounds in Deciphering ...said, according to Kirby, “are the remains of the material you command</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">ResearchGate&#x27;&#x27;A Message in a Bottle:&quot; Confounds in Deciphering ...said, according to Kirby, “are the remains of the material you commande...</span></span></span>

## How the FBI Teletype Frames the Object

One of the most important contemporary documents is the FBI Dallas Field Office teletype of 8 July 1947. The message reported that a purported flying disc had been recovered near Roswell and described it as a hexagonal object suspended from a balloon by a cable. The same communication noted that it resembled a high-altitude weather balloon with a radar reflector and that the object was being transferred for examination. <span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://vault.fbi.gov/Roswell%20UFO" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: vault.fbi.gov">[FBI+2JASON COLAVITO]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">vault.fbi.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Roswell UFOOn</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Roswell UFOOn July 8, 1947, the FBI Dallas Field Office sent a teletype regarding a “flying disc” that resembled a high altitude weath...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: July 8, 1947</span></span></span>

## Where Kirby’s Wording Sits Between Balloon and Body Claims

The most interesting aspect of Kirby’s interpretation is that it occupies a middle ground between competing Roswell narratives.

On one side are [readings]({{ 'readings/' | relative_url }}) of the memo that have been interpreted as supporting crash-victim scenarios.
