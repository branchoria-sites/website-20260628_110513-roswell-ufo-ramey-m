## Introduction

When the U.S. General Accounting Office (GAO, now the Government Accountability Office) investigated the Roswell case in the mid-1990s, it was not trying to resolve every claim about UFOs. Its narrower task was to determine what official government records still existed concerning the July 1947 incident and whether relevant records had been properly preserved. The result was striking: after searching across multiple agencies and archives, the GAO reported that only two known government records created in 1947 directly referred to the event. Those records were a July 1947 history report from the Roswell unit and the FBI's 8 July 1947 teletype message. <span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.gao.gov/products/nsiad-95-187" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: gao.gov">[GAO+2georgehbalazs.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">gao.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">nsiad 95 187</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GAOResults of a Search for Records Concerning the 1947 ...GAO provided information on the 1947 weather balloon crash at Roswell Army Air...</span></span></span>

For researchers assessing the Roswell paper trail, this finding matters because it sharply narrows the pool of surviving contemporary evidence. ## The GAO Search for Roswell Records

The GAO inquiry began after Congressman Steven Schiff requested an investigation into the historical record surrounding the Roswell incident. The agency examined military archives, federal record repositories, and responses from numerous government organisations in an effort to identify surviving documentation from 1947. <span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.gao.gov/products/nsiad-95-187" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: gao.gov">[GAO+2muller.lbl.gov]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">gao.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">nsiad 95 187</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GAOResults of a Search for Records Concerning the 1947 ...GAO provided information on the 1947 weather balloon crash at Roswell Army Air...</span></span></span>

The search uncovered an important archival problem. The GAO found that several categories of Roswell Army Air Field records that might once have contained useful information no longer existed. Among the missing materials were administrative records covering March 1945 through December 1949 and outgoing message files covering October 1946 through December 1949. The GAO could determine that these records had been destroyed, but documentation explaining precisely who authorised the destruction, when it occurred, or under what authority was incomplete. <span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://ufotransparency.com/files/1995-07-28-gao-roswell-report-nsiad-95-187-gao-nsiad-95-187-roswell-1995" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ufotransparency.com">[UFO Transparency]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ufotransparency.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">1995 07 28 gao roswell report nsiad 95 187 gao nsiad 95 187 roswell 1995</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UFO TransparencyResults of a Search for Records Concerning the 1947 Crash...28 Jul 1995 — The GAO&#x27;s 1995 audit of federal records relate...</span></span></span>

At the same time, the investigation did not reveal a hidden cache of Roswell documents elsewhere in government. Searches by agencies including the CIA failed to locate records concerning the crash, and later archival reviews likewise noted an absence of Roswell-specific documentation in collections such as Project Blue Book files. <span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://ufotransparency.com/files/1995-07-28-gao-roswell-report-nsiad-95-187-gao-nsiad-95-187-roswell-1995" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ufotransparency.com">[UFO Transparency]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ufotransparency.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">1995 07 28 gao roswell report nsiad 95 187 gao nsiad 95 187 roswell 1995</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UFO TransparencyResults of a Search for Records Concerning the 1947 Crash...28 Jul 1995 — The GAO&#x27;s 1995 audit of federal records relate...</span></span></span>

Against that backdrop, the significance of the two surviving 1947 records increased considerably. They became the principal contemporaneous government documents available for evaluating what officials were recording at the time. <span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://georgehbalazs.com/wp-content/uploads/2020/02/1995-Results-of-a-search-for-records-concerning-the-1947-crash-near-Rosell-NM-GAO-Government-Records..pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: georgehbalazs.com">[georgehbalazs.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">georgehbalazs.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on georgehbalazs.com.</span></span></span>

## How the Teletype and Unit History Align

The first surviving record is the well-known FBI teletype sent from the Dallas field office on 8 July 1947. The message relayed information received from military sources and described the recovered object as a [hexagonal disc]({{ 'hexagonal-disc/' | relative_url }}) suspended from a balloon by cable, resembling a high-altitude weather balloon with a radar reflector. It also stated that the material was being sent for examination. <span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://vault.fbi.gov/Roswell%20UFO" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: vault.fbi.gov">[FBI+2GAO]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">vault.fbi.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Roswell UFOOn</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Roswell UFOOn July 8, 1947, the FBI Dallas Field Office sent a teletype regarding a “flying disc” that resembled a high altitude weath...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: July 8, 1947</span></span></span>

The second surviving record is less famous but equally important. According to the GAO, a July 1947 history report produced by the combined 509th Bomb Group and Roswell Army Air Field recorded that the base had recovered a reported "flying disc." The same history report further noted that subsequent investigation identified the object as a radar-tracking balloon. <span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://georgehbalazs.com/wp-content/uploads/2020/02/1995-Results-of-a-search-for-records-concerning-the-1947-crash-near-Rosell-NM-GAO-Government-Records..pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: georgehbalazs.com">[georgehbalazs.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">georgehbalazs.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on georgehbalazs.com.</span></span></span>

## What Missing Records Cannot Prove by Themselves

The destruction of Roswell-era records has often been treated as evidence of a cover-up. The GAO did conclude that important records were missing and that the available documentation concerning their destruction was unsatisfactory. Those findings remain historically significant. [UFO Transparency](https://ufotransparency.com/files/1995-07-28-gao-roswell-report-nsiad-95-187-gao-nsiad-95-187-roswell-1995)

However, the absence of records does not by itself establish what those records contained. Missing message logs cannot automatically be treated as proof of an extraterrestrial craft, nor can they be assumed to have supported the balloon explanation. Once records are gone, their contents become a matter of inference rather than direct evidence. [GAO](https://www.gao.gov/products/nsiad-95-187)

This distinction is crucial when evaluating the surviving paper trail. Historians generally give greater weight to extant contemporary documents than to speculation about documents that no longer exist. In the Roswell case, the two surviving 1947 records are therefore unusually important because they are not reconstructed memories or later interpretations; they are original government records produced during the relevant period. [GAO+2georgehbalazs.com](https://www.gao.gov/products/nsiad-95-187)

The missing records leave unanswered questions about exactly how information moved through military channels in July 1
